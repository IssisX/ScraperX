## Identity-critical signals

### 1. The tower reads as a machine before it reads as a building

The exterior must present dense, legible, life-size machinery integrated into the load-bearing architecture: hoists, drums, gears, cranes, lifts, gantries, counterweights, rails, carriers, pipework, catwalks, structural frames, suspended loads, and related mechanisms. These systems should occupy real volume and create traversal space, occlusion, hazards, access, and route choices.

Avoid a skyscraper shell with small decorative mechanisms pasted onto it.

### 2. Monumental mass, not fragile lattice

Primary structure should feel heavy enough to carry industrial loads: deep steel sections, concrete masses, timber/wood infill where appropriate, thick platforms, machine housings, bridgeways, towers, drums, wheels, and lifting frames. Fine truss detail may exist, but the dominant read is **massive load-bearing construction**, not spaghetti scaffolding.

### 3. Exterior traversal is braided through machinery

Ordinary first-person routes should pass beside, under, over, around, and eventually through working mechanisms. Catwalks, bridgeways, loading platforms, maintenance stairs, crane structures, elevator rails, machine voids, and open structural frames should make the exterior itself playable.

Interior spaces should emerge naturally from route and machine access rather than replacing the exterior as the default experience.

### 4. Vertical scale is finished by atmosphere, not by showing the crown

At grade and lower elevations, the player should rarely receive a clean, complete silhouette of the tower. Steam, mist, weather, thin cloud, stack plume, glare, depth haze, structural overlap, and perspective should truncate the visible ascent so the tower continues beyond what the eye can resolve.

Composition should favor low-angle upward views, long vertical continuities, overlapping machine layers, and distant moving silhouettes.

### 5. Material color, not neon color

The palette comes from industrial materials and weathering:

- mill-scale and dark rolled steel;
- oxidized steel and rust bloom;
- poured and patched concrete;
- weathered timber and wood infill;
- galvanized mesh and guardrail;
- grease, tar, wet asphalt, soot, grime, and runoff;
- faded warning yellow;
- chipped hazard orange;
- dull red-brown paint or signage where physically justified.

No synthwave edge glow, universal emissive trims, candy-colored machinery, or neon used as the dominant identity.

### 6. Light must have a source

Warm light is sparse and local: sodium/amber work lamps, caged fixtures, floodlights, maintenance bulbs, control panels, and weld/process glow only when an actual source exists. Daylight and weather provide the broad field; practicals carve localized warm pockets through cool steel/concrete mass.

Lighting should reveal volume, structure, moisture, and machine depth rather than color-code the world like an arcade cabinet.

### 7. Mechanical density must remain legible

The reference image is dense without becoming visual noise because large systems remain readable as distinct masses. ScraperX should layer machinery at multiple scales while preserving causal readability: the player can identify what turns, lifts, carries, tensions, opens, counterweights, or blocks.

Complexity should come from **coupled readable systems**, not indiscriminate prop clutter.

## Rejection tests

Reject a presentation direction if it makes ScraperX read primarily as:

- a clean sci-fi tower;
- a neon cyberpunk structure;
- a generic modern office skyscraper;
- a thin scaffold maze;
- a sequence of enclosed rooms with exterior skybox decoration;
- a cinematic matte painting whose machinery cannot be entered or affected;
- a small mechanism test arena scaled up cosmetically;
- a tower whose crown is cleanly visible from the opening approach.

## Application rule

When adding new exterior content, ask: **does this increase the sense that one enormous inhabited industrial machine is carrying real loads upward through weather, structure, and mechanism while remaining traversable by a human body?**

If not, it is probably decoration rather than identity.
