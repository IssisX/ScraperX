# SCRAPERX — STORY AND PLAYER DIRECTION

**Authored campaign:** The Common Way  
**Owner direction:** 2026-10-20; define a substantial story, real opening, purpose, and continuing player direction.  
**Authority boundary:** Narrative content under the governing laws and GDD. The [Ascent Atlas](02_ASCENT_ATLAS.md) owns physical placement. Native world state owns what happened. This document defines fiction and intended encounters; it does not establish that their characters, places, dialogue, or complete route exist in the game.

## 1. The story

**A former access rigger climbs the city their own survey erased, turning improvised ways up into a shared way home.**

The tower is called **the Exchange**. It began as a vertical freight interchange: receiving yards, fabrication floors, stores, machine halls, and a construction terminus that kept moving upward. Its backers promised a complete industrial city. They built the frame to 1,600 metres, sold its future several times, and left before finishing the handover.

The lower and middle decks did become a city. Workers stayed. Families occupied former project accommodation. A machine landing became a street; the space under a goods platform became a workshop. Temporary service routes acquired names and regular users. Some places are busy and warm. Others are enormous, nearly empty working spaces that no sensible person crosses without a reason. The Exchange is neither a deserted ruin nor a building where every floor is an apartment block.

The player is **Rowan “Ro” Vale**, an access rigger raised around Receiving. Ro left for outside work after years of promising to come back when the tower was finished. Their older sister Iona stayed and now runs the receiving yard.

Ro once signed a survey withdrawing temporary construction access above occupied work areas. The survey described where a contractor could safely send a crew. During the failed handover, its blank areas became “unoccupied” on the closure map. The latest dismantling notice carries Ro's signature beside places where people still live. Ro did not knowingly sign away a city. Ro did stop looking at what happened after the paperwork left their hands.

The reuse did not stop at the signature. A company continuity program later supplied the handover with a living representative who looks like Ro. Meeting that person is the campaign's early human mystery, developed in §9. It reveals a deliberate attempt to make the false record look witnessed; it does not turn Ro into its knowing author.

Iona sends the notice with one sentence: **“Come look at what you signed.”** Ro returns expecting an argument, a correction, and a short paid job.

The immediate problem is physical. The residents' common freight spine still shares its return assembly with the abandoned construction plant at the Crown. The final dismantling pass would remove the construction equipment and strand the retained service system. Local machinery can still work on its own supply; there is no magical tower-wide blackout. What the city lacks is a dependable shared way to move people, food, tools, and heavy repair stock between its separated districts.

The last separation has to happen at the Crown, on the actual service deck at 1,600 metres. It involves real suspended loads, retained supports, and machinery that cannot safely be reconfigured from a downstairs terminal. The ordinary access route stopped being ordinary years ago. Ro has to climb through the tower that exists, rather than the empty drawing they helped leave behind.

At first Ro climbs to correct a name. By the end, they climb because a city needs someone to finish a piece of work and stay responsible for the result.

## 2. What drives the ascent

**First objective:** reach Freight One, the first supported tower ring at +11 m, from the existing receiving yard.

**Campaign objective:** physically reach the Crown service deck at 1,600 m and separate the residents' retained freight system from the construction plant being retired.

The summit is the far end of an understandable job, visible long before it is reachable. Its importance comes from where the shared physical connection terminates, rather than a radio that arbitrarily works only at the top. Radios can carry conversation where a real connection is available. They cannot carry a suspended load or seat an isolated return assembly.

The story has pressure without a running apocalypse clock. Dismantling is planned work, held at a safe boundary; it does not secretly advance while the player explores, falls, or uses a slower route. There is no off-screen machine that cuts the tower apart after an invented number of minutes. The cost of delay is a place continuing to live with poor access, not a dishonest timer.

Getting higher is always a valid player choice. Restoring useful access for other people gives that choice weight. Ro can use a daring personal route without pretending it is now safe for a loaded forklift or another inhabitant. **A route a climber can use and a route a neighborhood can use are different achievements.** Neither invalidates the other.

## 4. The campaign in five acts

Acts describe changes in understanding and purpose. They are not five mandatory height gates, a new list of tower coordinates, or a requirement to operate every existing machine in order. Physical skips change which information the player meets first. They do not make the story reject a legitimate ascent.

### Act I — Empty on Paper

**Place:** Receiving and the first freight ring. The existing forklift, independent cargo, dispatch-height gallery, crane, and tower approach provide the opening material.

Ro comes home through the working end of the tower. Iona's first request is modest: get to Freight One and look up from inside the frame. There is no terminal that unlocks movement. The player can immediately drive, climb, inspect the yard, approach the crane, or find another physical way in.

The yard makes the conflict concrete before explaining it. A dismantling notice says **CLEAR FOR REMOVAL**. Beside it, an active receiving board still lists ordinary deliveries: flour, pump seals, school chairs. These are authored environmental details to add, not cargo-fetch objectives. The same place is empty in one record and busy in another.

Freight One changes the scale of the request. Iona explains the shared construction return at the Crown. Ro can leave the signature argument for later: “Then we separate it first.” The campaign direction becomes the summit. The first act ends with a decision made while the player is standing somewhere they reached by their own actions.

**Dramatic turn:** Ro intended to correct a record. The record is attached to a machine that still has people depending on it.

### Act II — The Ways That Remained

**Places:** the lower service routes, exposed structure, working hoist spaces, and the first inhabited threshold. Existing authored lower machinery and parkour supply physical material; the inhabited threshold requires new authorship.

Ro discovers that the supposedly closed routes have not simply been abandoned. People repaired around lost stairs, moved goods to different landings, and learned the frame from the outside. Nadi's annotations disagree with Ro's old memory in useful, specific ways.

A forklift is valuable because a climber cannot shoulder a pallet or place a stable heavy foothold with wishful thinking. A crane matters because a useful vehicle downstairs cannot help a district upstairs. A line matters because an accessible control or receiving shelf may lie across a void. Parkour joins those places. The grappling gun is a tool Ro already carries, not a sacred object awarded after three errands.

The player sees two scales of success: reaching a ring personally and leaving a freight connection that could carry more than one agile person. Optional repairs must change actual access or capability. No one asks for six scattered parts just to justify time in the district.

Nadi initially expects Ro to disappear after the paid bit. Trust grows through observed work and honest admission, rather than a dialogue choice that adds friendship points. Ro finally says what the original survey meant. Nadi answers: “It told your crew where to stop. They used it to say we'd all left.”

**Dramatic turn:** the tower was not secretly finished and then ruined. Its people have been completing a different tower inside the unfinished one.

During the first fifth of the authored game, an archived Ro statement is followed by the actual meeting with Ari. This is part of Act II's discovery that the record and the place disagree, not a late-game twist replacing the ascent. §9 specifies the pacing, encounter, and continued payoff.

### Act III — The City in the Gaps

**Place:** an authored inhabited district where industrial scale and everyday use occupy the same space. This is future content, not a claim about current NPCs or housing.

This is the emotional center, with room to breathe after exposed ascent. A market uses the shelter beneath a stationary goods deck. Work lights illuminate repaired tables. Someone measures door frames against a crate they cannot get upstairs. The common freight connection is no longer an abstract engineering request.

Nadi calls the district **the Seam**: the line where the company's finished drawing and everyone's unfinished lives failed to meet. The name belongs to this place; it does not rename an existing source encounter or reserve an arbitrary altitude.

Ro finds successive handover sheets using the same survey outline. Each later revision removed uncertainty without anyone inspecting the missing places. Ro's signature was reused; Iona did not forge the problem to bring them home. There is no single concealed saboteur to defeat. The tower accumulated decisions whose consequences someone else was expected to own.

Ari's account exposes the later deliberate step: a presentation of the source survey became a purported first-person inspection. They offer evidence of the work identity and of a life before it, on their own terms. Ro can confront the person wearing their face without pretending that Ari invented the closure plan or that being employed excuses repeating it.

Contact with Dev produces the apparent setback. He will not reconnect the shared plant just because Ro has found residents. He asks the correct question: “What is the return carrying now?” Ro cannot yet answer. The next work is to establish and alter real load paths, not to win an argument on the radio.

**Dramatic turn:** proving people exist is necessary, but it does not make a dangerous reconnection safe. The player must make the proposed future work.

### Act IV — The Last Temporary Works

**Places:** upper construction frames, exposed maintenance access, large handling plant, and the approach to the Crown. These spaces and their complete connected ascent still require authorship.

The closer Ro gets to the original terminus, the less the building resembles a finished monument. Temporary work platforms carry permanent labels. A weathered board still promises a passenger lift that was never commissioned. The huge view is both beautiful and embarrassing: the people below made homes while the project above kept announcing progress.

The most substantial encounters combine scale and access. Rigging changes what is held; a machine moves what a person cannot; parkour reaches the next useful position; the player returns to inspect what the operation actually did. An optional haul or shortcut may leave a better route for later use. These are consequences with a purpose, not separate tool tutorials.

Dev's presence becomes more legible. He has kept the upper plant in a stable held condition because the planned dismantling lacks a defensible retained configuration. Ro has found the missing use below; Dev has the missing limits above. Neither can finish the job alone by issuing instructions. Their agreement concerns an actual safe configuration that the player still has to make.

Ro's personal turn is quiet: they stop calling it “your tower.” After an operation leaves real retained support, they say, “Our side's held.” No forced speech, oath, or new hero title is needed.

**Dramatic turn:** the climb is no longer a protest against the handover. It is the missing handover, performed for the people who will live with it.

### Act V — The Common Way

**Place:** a supported service deck at the actual 1,600 m destination, with a clear view of both the final working assembly and the inhabited tower below. Summit and finale geometry are future authorship.

The final encounter is the campaign's idea at its clearest scale. The retired construction load must be held by real retained support before its shared connection can be separated. The residents' service return must remain in a usable configuration. The player reaches controls, inspects the actual held state, makes the separation, and observes retained service motion. The exact assembly must be engineered at its native owners; this story does not prescribe a new solver, fuel system, or unsupported mechanism.

There is no final boss and no console that turns the whole city green. A successful operation frees the shared freight system from the retired construction leg. It does not magically repair every district the player skipped. The world retains damage, alternate routes, local repairs, and unfinished jobs honestly.

**The main ascent is complete only when Ro physically stands on supported summit geometry at 1,600 m.** The handover resolution additionally requires the real final machine outcome. Reaching a trigger volume, reading Dev's last message, or seeing the summit from below cannot substitute for either condition.

After the actual separation and a demonstrated retained service movement, the ending can play while the player remains in control:

**Dev:** “Construction return isolated. The service side is yours.”  
**Iona:** “Ours, Dev. You live here too.”  
**Ro:** “Put that on the drawing.”

A corrected handover sheet now names the retained service connection **COMMON WAY — OPEN**. It records the state actually achieved; it is not the thing that causes it. The final view is down into a place the player now understands, with an ordinary freight movement doing more emotional work than fireworks.

The ending resolves Ro's responsibility and the central infrastructure problem. Exploration can continue in the persistent tower. Any return transport must exist and function physically; credits do not teleport the player downstairs or restore machines they damaged.

## 6. Story as the player's guide

The guide answers three questions in plain language: **Why does this matter? Where am I trying to get? What useful thing can I try here?** It does not supply an invisible correct sequence.

- **Keep purpose stable.** “Reach the Crown and free the common freight connection” survives a different route, a fall, or an optional repair. Local objectives explain the nearest useful opportunity without erasing that purpose.
- **Name destinations and results.** Prefer “Stand on Freight One” or “Leave the load supported at the receiving deck” to “Use forklift, then use crane.” A player who makes the result another way gets the result.
- **Let the world teach technique.** Load shapes, receiving space, actual controls, motion, support, signs, sounds, and short contextual cues should make an operation legible. Dialogue cannot compensate for an unusable receiver or invisible route.
- **Separate arrival from service.** A supported personal arrival can finish a traversal objective. A freight objective needs the actual specified vehicle/load at its real receiver. A loaded route can remain unfinished after Ro takes a shortcut.
- **Respect present knowledge.** Iona can explain Receiving; Nadi can explain discovered routes; Dev can explain the held upper plant. Nobody announces an unseen success because a mission flag advanced.
- **Make silence normal.** Trigger dialogue from a new meaningful situation. Suppress or defer it during acute falls and precise machine work. Do not restart the same line whenever the player enters a radius.
- **Accept different orders.** Reveal essential facts when first relevant; optional discoveries enrich them. A player reaching the Crown early must be able to learn the finale's actual constraints there without collecting earlier conversation tokens.
- **Make recovery useful.** After a miss, describe actual position or an available return route. Do not say “try again” while the pouch, crane, cargo, or line is still elsewhere. A survived lower landing is a new situation, not a lecture trigger.
- **Do not guilt the player for exploring.** Characters have wants; they do not invent imminent deaths or accuse the player of neglect for taking an allowed route. Optional work earns a visible consequence, not moral punishment for declining it.
- **Keep records truthful.** A narrative memory records that a message was heard or an outcome observed. It cannot create footing, power, isolation, inhabitants, or machinery motion. Restart/debug arrival is not an earned campaign ascent.

Useful continuing copy patterns are deliberately small:

| Need | Example |
|---|---|
| Purpose | **The common line ends at the Crown. Keep climbing.** |
| A nearby physical blocker | **The receiving shelf is above reach. Get a supported way onto it.** |
| Machinery opportunity | **The hoist can move the load. Reach its actual controls.** |
| Real incomplete state | **The load is still hanging. Find supported footing before releasing it.** |
| An accepted bypass | **You're above the old route. Keep going.** |
| Useful recovery | **You have firm footing here. Find an onward route or return to the last working machine.** |

These are writing patterns, not unconditional prompts. For example, “The load is still hanging” requires a real hanging-load observation. Do not guess that state from the story chapter.

## 8. Adult voice: people who know where to hurt

**Owner tone correction:** the humorous dark roast comes from Iona and Ro knowing each other, not a commentator describing a button press. A swear about the parking brake would fit almost any game. Iona keeping Ro as her emergency contact belongs to this relationship. That is the standard.

Their banter carries affection, resentment, and the embarrassing familiarity of family. Iona remembers how good Ro was at the job and how often other people handled what came after. Ro knows when she is using anger to avoid asking them to stay. Both can answer back. Neither delivers a polished stand-up routine, and Iona does not exist to administer an uninterrupted verbal beating.

**The narrative voice lives in their relationship.** There is no separate omniscient roast host. Ro's internal commentary is optional and earns its place only when it reveals something comparably personal; generic reaction barks are not the humor model. An exchange belongs to a reunion, a revealing piece of paperwork, or a change in trust. It is not a reward for reaching a coordinate or a punishment for mishandling a machine.

Real profanity and vulgarity are welcome when these people would actually use them. No coy substitute curses, compulsory swearing, protected-class slurs, or hate. Aim the sting at an evasion, broken promise, or choice they actually know about. Preserve the distinction between Ro's absence and the company's dishonest use of the access survey: **Ro did not knowingly declare an occupied city empty.** Iona can be furious without rewriting that fact.

Do not invent a dead parent, lost child, or fatal accident to buy emotional weight. The existing relationship already has something ugly and useful in it: Iona kept making space for someone who could leave. A claim that Ro died must not create a new bereavement subplot or become a cheap “you'd miss your own funeral” punchline. Dark does not mean piling up tragedies; it means letting an intimate joke expose what being absent cost someone else.
