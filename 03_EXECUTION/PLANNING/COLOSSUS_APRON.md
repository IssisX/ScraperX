# The apron chain

The old ascent plans are withdrawn. They stacked small, hard-to-see parts and called that a lift. Those plans are not the build.

This is the first machine, on the ground, east of where you are born. One chain. Every part is big enough to read from where you stand.

**Built and proven so far:** the pipes sit on the ramp, and the wedge is the only thing holding them. You drive the ball into the wedge and it slides out. The pipes run. If you do not, the wedge stays and the door above stays shut.

**Not proven:** the pipes do not land in the scale yet, so the door, the boulder, the hanging ball, the slabs and the seesaw are standing there and do not move. The seesaw does not lift you yet. That handoff is the next piece of this same chain, not a new idea.

The native simulation owns the bodies. Godot only draws them. There is no joint in the presentation, and no motor.

## What you do, and what lifts you

You shove a heavy ball into a wedge. A stack of pipes rolls. They tip a scale. The scale pulls a beam out of a trapdoor. A boulder falls, hits a wrecking ball, and the ball knocks over a line of slabs. The last slab drops on the short end of a seesaw. You are standing on the long end. It rises about 12 m.

If you are not on it, it still rises. The fallen slabs are the climb, and they are not a stair.

## What was thrown out of the draft, and why

The draft this came from ran eleven beats and ended in a gong. Most of that never lifted a person.

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| Newton's cradle | It only looks right if the middle balls do not move. A real hit shuffles them. That is a trick, not a lift. |
| The spool, the ring, the wooden chock | A small ring shot off an axle. That is the same hidden-part problem. |
| The scissor jack and its springs | Folded links and stored spring force you cannot see. |
| The puck fired 100 m up a rail | A second tower, and a guide doing the work. |
| Five long switchback ramps | Ramps. Also the player would try to walk them. The ball does not need a maze. One curved chute is enough. |
| A drawbridge as the ending | A door is not a ride. The seesaw is the ride. |

## The beats

Masses are the ones the build has to hit. If a lighter body would not win, it is the wrong body.

1. **Pipes.** Sixteen steel pipes on a slope, about 200 kg each. One steel wedge under the bottom pipe. You roll an 80 kg ball into the wedge. The wedge is big. You can see it leave. The pipes roll off the lip.

2. **Scale.** A beam on a center hinge, a basket each end. The right basket holds a 2 000 kg block and is down. The pipes (about 3 200 kg) land in the left basket. The left side goes down. The right side rises and its hook pulls a steel beam out of the trapdoor. The beam is the pin. You can see the hole it leaves.

3. **Trapdoor.** A floor about 6 m on a side, hinged at one edge, held up only by that beam. A 4 000 kg boulder sits on it. The beam comes out. The floor swings down. The boulder drops onto one curved chute. Not a ramp you can walk.

4. **Ball.** A 2 500 kg iron sphere on a rigid arm, hanging still at the bottom of the chute. The boulder hits it. The sphere swings.

5. **Slabs.** Five concrete slabs, each about 6 m tall and 8 000 kg, standing, spaced so a falling one reaches the next. The swinging sphere hits the first. They fall in a line.

6. **Seesaw.** A beam, hinge closer to one end. Short end starts about 4 m up. Long end starts on the ground, with a platform. The last slab lands on the short end and drives it down. The long end rises about 12 m. You ride the platform.

Energy, checked from the bodies, not from a script: the last slab gives up far more than the platform and a person gain. 8 000 kg falling a few metres is hundreds of kilojoules. A 500 kg platform plus a person, lifted 12 m, is about 60 kJ. The rest is the hit and the slab staying down.

## What "it worked" means

- The wedge still in place: you can pull and shove all day, and the platform does not rise.
- The ball knocks the wedge out, and you are on the platform: you are about 12 m higher, standing on that platform, because the slab went down.
- You missed the platform: the slabs are on the ground and you can climb them. No stair, no ramp, no ladder required. A ladder may sit beside it for someone who does not want to figure the slabs out.

## What this does not do yet

It does not replace the machines already built higher in the tower. Those stay until this chain is in and proven. The next chain, after this one lifts you, is another chain of the same kind, not a return to pins and screws.
