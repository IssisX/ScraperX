# The second tower in play: proposal (2026-10-21)

Nothing here is built. It is the source-grounded proposal the owner asked for before the
expansion is committed (00_START_HERE.md, row of 2026-10-21).

**How it was made.** Three mappers read the code first. Their map, with file and line evidence for
every fact, is `verified_map.json`. The lead then checked its load-bearing facts in the code: the
parachute, the lethal speed, the wind, the gaps, the buried foot and the hook. A blind panel was
started next (three designers and three refuters per design), but the owner stopped it to save
usage. **The three approaches and their attacks below are the lead's own, through the same three
lenses (physics and rules, touch and gameplay, code and phone). They are not independent
refuters.** The panel's script is kept and can be run again.

**What the map found that nobody knew.** The parachute's glide is about 0.66 off a walk, 1.06 off
a sprint and 1.25 at flow speed (forward distance per metre of drop). These are computed, not run.
So tower two can probably already be reached **by air, and only from high on tower one**, onto
dead-end pier and duct tops at 460 and 390 m. Today that crossing just ends on a ledge with
nowhere to go.

overhead, it "read as a black void hanging over the stack". At 52-66 m it is a facade across a
street, lit by its floor bands. That has to be seen in a still before anything else is built.

## 3. Three approaches

### A. The swing across (built by the climber)

A crew jib sticks 30 m out from tower one's deck. The climber hooks the bar at its tip from the
deck's edge, 30 m back, and steps off. They swing down under the jib and up to tower two's face,
60 m away at the same height (a 30 m pendulum's same-height span is 2 × 30 m), arriving almost
still, and catch a rail there.

| Lens | My attack |
|---|---|
| Physics and rules | Honest: 24.3 m/s at the bottom (√(2 × 9.81 × 30)), a line pull of 3 body weights = 2.47 kN, under the 6 kN hold. **But the end of the arc rises almost straight up along the far face**, so a platform there is hit from below, and a lone rail at the apex has to sit exactly on the arc. |
| Touch and gameplay | Breathtaking once. But aiming a 30 m shot at a thin bar needs 0.3-0.65° (2-4 units of thumb drag), and the HUD has only a dot. A missed catch swings back to tower one, which is a natural retry. |
| Code and phone | Small: one jib, one rail. It needs the move (no 30 m line crosses 100 m). |
| Scores (1-10) | wow 9 · physics 7 · touch 4 · rules 7 · buildable 6 |

### B. The ward bridge (the crew's machine)

Tower two carries the crew's old climbing-formwork decks, one every 22 m up its face, and at the
top a bridge house with a half-raised drawbridge leaf. The first visit is by canopy from high on
tower one, then up tower two's face with the hook. Out on the leaf, the climber's weight brings it
down onto tower one's summit deck, where it stays as a permanent walkway.

| Lens | My attack |
|---|---|
| Physics and rules | Gliding gates the first visit with no flag: tower two's decks begin at 640 m, and a glide loses a metre of height for every 0.66-1.25 m across. The leaf is a balanced bascule held by its bearing friction, so a climber's weight far enough out tips it, and a damper governs its fall (numbers in section 4). **Rule 9 risk**: a leaf that goes down under weight is kin to the bay's leaf and the derrick's load-luffing. |
| Touch and gameplay | Every step is an existing verb: run, CHUTE, steer, HOOK and hold to reel, CLIMB UP, walk. Each failure has a way back (section 4). Aiming the hook 22 m straight up at three rails gives a ~2.9° window, workable by thumb. The glide is skill with a forgiving catch. |
| Code and phone | The move, then new static decks, a hinged leaf with a counterweight and a damper, appended after the bay so old saves still load. Kept as world solids, the decks draw far, in batches. |
| Scores (1-10) | wow 8 · physics 8 · touch 7 · rules 7 · buildable 6 |

## 4. Recommended first slice: the ward bridge (B), with two grafts

**Roles.** Tower one is **the machines**: the steel frame and its chain of rides, which the player
already knows. Tower two is **the face**: a sheer concrete core climbed with the hook, deck by deck,
in the open, where the hook stops being a toy and becomes the way up. The crossing is the canopy
the first time, and after that a bridge the player built themselves.

**The slice, step by step** (tower two moved: face z -228; decks on its face at 640, 662, 684,
706, 728, 750, 772 and 794 m, each 3 m deep with three rails at its edge; the bridge house at 803 m):

| # | Where | What the player does | The physics | If it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tower one, the 750 deck's -z edge (750.1 m) or the 728 deck | Run at the edge, vault the 0.8 m parapet, CHUTE | Take-off speed sets the glide: from 750.1 m across 66 m, a walk-off arrives at 650 m, a sprint at 688 m, flow speed at 697 m (height − 66 / glide) | Short: the 640 deck catches arrivals down to 640 m. Lower still: the canopy lands you at the foot (alive, 9 m/s) and the climb starts again; the slingshot takes you to the 220 ring |
| 2 | Tower two's face, decks 662-684 | Steer onto a deck, or into the face just above one | Sink 7.1-8.2 m/s (under the 12 m/s ankle break). Into the face at 5.4-7.9 m/s horizontal: felt, under the 10 m/s that kills | Over 8.5 m/s, a flow-speed glide straight in breaks an arm (half grip for 30 s), so steer across, not straight in. The canopy stays open against the face, and you settle onto the deck below |
| 3 | Up the face, 662 → 794 m | Stand at a deck's back, aim up at the next deck's rails 22 m above, HOOK, hold to reel, CLIMB UP | Reel 3 m/s with 1.2 kN against your 0.82 kN weight: 22 m in 7.3 s. The hands catch the rail lip (they still catch while on the hook), and CLIMB UP mantles | A miss: the hook comes back within 2.5 s, so fire again. A fall: the canopy, and you glide back toward tower one (from 780 m, across 66 m, you arrive at 680-727 m and can settle on its 640-684 decks), or die and wake on the last deck |
| 4 | The bridge house, 803 m | Walk out and up the half-raised leaf (39 m long, raised 35°, a walkable deck: 35° is under the 56° walkable limit) | Your weight's lever on the pivot is 84 × 9.81 × s × cos 35° = 675 N per metre out. Against a 15 kNm bearing friction, it begins to sink when you are 22 m out. A damper caps it at 0.1 rad/s: 6.1 s down, with the tip moving at most 3.9 m/s | Turn back before 22 m and nothing moves. Fall off: CHUTE. From 20 m out on the leaf (about 814 m), tower one's decks are about 50 m across and 64-86 m down, so even a walking-speed glide (0.66) reaches the 728 deck |
| 5 | The leaf, now level | Walk its 39 m to tower one's summit deck (802.64 m; the tip seats 2.5 m onto the deck) | A balanced leaf with nobody on it stays where it is; a catch at the summit seat latches it (an existing saved catch) | None needed. You are at the summit, by a second route |

**Why it is earned, by architecture alone.** Tower two has no openings and no holds below 640 m,
and its foot is in the rock. To reach its lowest deck (640 m) by glide you must start at
640 + 66 / 1.25 = 693 m or higher. That means tower one's 706 deck (strip), 728 or 750 deck, or
higher: about 12 minutes of climbing. No flag, no wall: just height, the glide, and a face with
nothing to grip below its decks.

**The playable choices.**
- Where to take off, and how fast: the run-up sets which deck you reach.
- Which deck to land on, and how close to the face you dare go.
- **The braid**: the crown gondola and the derrick to the summit as before, or tower two's face and the bridge.
- How far out on the leaf to walk before it moves under you.
- After the bridge is down: the summit becomes a junction, tower two above 803 m is the onward route, and the bridge is the way back across.

**The earned shortcut.** The lowered leaf stays down through deaths, saves and loads (a latched
kit catch, already saved). Later visits walk from the summit straight to tower two's bridge house.

**Grafts.**
- From A: the swing becomes the **next** slice's crossing, lower down, where the gap after the move is 52-59 m (the 154 m deck to the 330 ring). That is the weave: a second, lower crossing to tower two. Its end-of-arc problem (section 3, A) is still unsolved, so it is a direction, not a design.
- From C, story only: the leaf is the old **ward bridge** and the bridge house was the ward's end, so her chart's pages can be found there (her existing page system, with new spots). She herself does not come across in this slice.

Both are rechecked only by me (the second needs no new physics). The swing graft keeps every
objection listed under A.

## 5. Surviving objections (open unless marked)

1. **The look of the move**: the "black void" history. Needs a still from the yard and from the 750 deck through the Godot MCP, which needs the owner to approve `run_project`.
2. **The glide is computed, not run.** The native gate test (section 6) settles it.
3. **Rule 9**: a leaf lowered by weight is kin to the bay and the derrick. The owner's call: keep it (different form and look), or give the leaf another start.
4. **One mandatory glide once tower two is the way on above 803 m.** Plan 2.2 forbids mandatory launches across a gap. The bridge removes the glide for every later visit, but not the first. A stated delta: the price of "earned".
5. **Hook aim on the phone**: the HUD shows only a dot. Three rails per deck give ~2.9°; it may still want a small hold marker.
6. **Dead-end ledges** (pier tops at 460 m, duct tops at 390 m) stay reachable by glide. Not a dead save (the canopy reaches the ground), but a long way back. Option: slope their tops.
7. **Her chart names places by height.** A tower-two deck at 684 m would be written as "the 684 m deck", which breaks accurate-or-silent. Her chart needs tower-two names.
8. **Arm break on a fast straight-in arrival** (8.5-8.9 m/s at flow speed). Accepted as a consequence, or the decks reach farther out.
9. **Phone budget**: about 8 decks of steel plus a leaf, against 390 of 700 draw calls at perf_top. Measured locally only.

## 6. The smallest decisive playable test

Build only:
1. The move: the native box, the drawn box, the relief's `face_z`, the regenerated solids, and tower two's own entity id.
2. Five decks on its face, at 640, 662, 684, 706 and 728 m, with their rails.

Then run two checks:
- **Native gate test**: from the 750 deck's edge, a sprint-off with the canopy lands alive on one of tower two's decks. From TP-640 (640.25 m), the same never reaches tower two's lowest deck.
- **Touch scenario from the Deck750 spawn**: run off, CHUTE, land on tower two, then make two hook pitches up its face (for a sprint-off: 684 → 706 → 728). It reports the landing speed, the aim attempts per pitch and the time.

If the glide and the pitches feel right on the owner's phone, build the rest: the decks to 794 m,
the bridge house and the leaf. If they don't, the idea dies cheaply, before the leaf exists.

## 7. Decisions for the owner

1. Move tower two to a 52-66 m gap (recommended), or keep it at 110-131 m and cross by canopy only, with a much longer leaf.
2. The ward bridge (B) as the first slice, at the top of the route (recommended), or start lower.
3. The leaf's start: the climber's weight (recommended; a rule 9 risk), or another physical act.
4. Her tower: story now, and her body crossing later, or not at all.
