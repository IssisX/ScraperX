## A. Preserve moving-support momentum through footing and departure

**Classification: production-established.** Complete ScraperX route/device acceptance remains separately scoped.

**Problem and owner.** Riding the wheel cabin, cart or AS-026 swing must retain translation and rotation at the feet. Passive hand release must preserve already acquired momentum. `src/sim/simulation.cpp:1265` owns `SupportSample`; contact classification at `:1455` rejects sensors and uses the actual manifold normal. Player creation at `:1980` is a dynamic capsule with translation-only DOFs, gravity and mass, rather than either Jolt character class. `:5649` owns ordinary finite push-off; the causal-support branch at `:5777` has its own push-off accounting. `tests/physical_traversal_tests.cpp:36` owns passive departure checks.

**Mathematics and assumptions.** At world contact point p, `v_s(p) = v_COM + omega × (p − c)`, with metres, m/s and rad/s. Relative locomotion uses `u = v_player − v_s(p)`. Ground classification `n_y ≥ 0.55` admits roughly 56.6 degrees from horizontal; admissibility does not guarantee enough traction to remain still. Passive departure retains world velocity; adding support velocity again would double-count motion. An active vertical impulse j (N·s) changes relative speed by `k j`, where k includes both bodies' translation and support rotation. Frozen-inertia actuator work is `u_y j + 0.5 k j²` in joules; braking and subsequent positive work must be separated when the trajectory crosses zero speed.

**Verified APIs and native approach.** Installed `BodyInterface.h:212,228–229` supplies `GetPointVelocity(BodyID,RVec3)` and COM/point `AddImpulse` overloads; `Body.h:176–179` delegates point velocity to `MotionProperties.h:143`. Keep these native owners, acquire actual support once per tick, apply an equal/opposite impulse to a dynamic receiver, and clear hand constraints without assigning a new departure velocity. Treat the ordinary and causal branches as distinct contracts during any accounting repair. Jolt's [pinned CharacterVirtual header](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/e77f175595e64cb44218cc9d9d56fc365ad0e36a/Jolt/Physics/Character/CharacterVirtual.h) has `ExtendedUpdate` with explicit filters and allocator; replacing the current rigid capsule would require re-proving its reciprocal machine interactions. `Character` is another alternative, not the present owner.

**Expected behavior, risks and cost.** A rotating support carries the feet faster farther from its axis; jump recoil affects a light receiver; passive release has no artificial speed reset. Edge cases include contact switching, steep faces, sensors, invalid BodyIDs, fixed-joint assemblies and simultaneous landing/jump. Record support identity, contact point, point velocity, approach velocity, work and first airborne velocity. Point sampling is small; assembly inverse mass can traverse joints and allocate, so measure that path before optimizing.

**Rejecting test.** The inspected passive-release test requires gravity-on two-hand climbing, releases above a receiver, then checks `v_y_after = v_y_before − 9.81/90` within 0.002 m/s and zero hand constraints. Rotating-contact checks in `tests/simulation_tests.cpp:841` compare point velocity with `omega × r`. Extend a real cart/wheel departure with unequal receiver mass and contact offset: reject doubled platform momentum or absent recoil, while allowing measured external gravity/contact impulses. A static-only jump test cannot reject either defect.

## B. Derive wheel/cart inertia from the mass that actually moves

**Classification: production-established.** A calibrated full machine energy ledger is additional work.

**Problem and owner.** A visually large wheel should resist angular acceleration according to mass distribution; cart deck, rollers and slab must exchange load rather than follow animated elevation. `src/sim/gravity_wheel_geometry.cpp:324` builds independent hanging buckets and the passive wheel bearing; `src/sim/gravity_cart_geometry.cpp:215` creates the 275 kg deck, four independent 35 kg rollers and 570 kg slab. `src/sim/mechanism_kit.cpp:200` owns body construction; `:267` installs mass properties and `:271` aggregates fixed-joint point inverse mass. `src/sim/supplied_ascent.cpp:38` computes finite cart impulse receipts.

**Mathematics and assumptions.** For each rigid component, `c = sum(m_i c_i)/sum(m_i)` and `I_c = sum(R_i I_i R_iᵀ + m_i[(d_i·d_i)1 − d_i d_iᵀ])`, with inertia in kg·m² and `d_i = c_i − c`. World inertia is `R I_body Rᵀ`. Newton–Euler gives `m dv/dt = F` and `I dω/dt + ω × (Iω) = τ`. Linear and angular energy are `0.5 m|v|²` and `0.5 ω·Iω` joules. A thin ring's `I_axis ≈ mr²` is only a diagnostic approximation; hanging scoops/cabin are separate bodies and must not be merged into a single rigid ring. Fixed-joint cluster inertia is an approximation to its constrained instantaneous response, not every articulated mechanism's effective mass.

**Verified APIs and native approach.** Installed `MassProperties.h:42,48,51` supplies `ScaleToMass`, `Rotate`, `Translate`; `MotionProperties.h:102,140` supplies `SetMassProperties(EAllowedDOFs,const MassProperties&)` and `MultiplyWorldSpaceInverseInertiaByVector(Quat,Vec3)`. `Body.h:209` supplies world inverse inertia. Keep shape-derived mass/inertia at construction, inspect its principal moments before accepting authored hulls, and use the existing kit ownership for any deliberate density correction. The [pinned MotionProperties header](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/e77f175595e64cb44218cc9d9d56fc365ad0e36a/Jolt/Physics/Body/MotionProperties.h) defines these installed operations. Per-body masses are simpler than detailed material density; a lumped ring is cheaper but loses independent bucket/passenger reactions. Prescribing wheel angle or deck height defeats load-dependent behavior.

**Expected behavior, risks and cost.** Off-axis mass increases rotational resistance; rider/slab weight changes motion; rollers turn under real rail contact. Bad units, shape-origin/COM confusion, non-positive principal moments and double-counted attached mass cause implausible response. Log COM, principal inertia, velocities, applied impulse, bank debit and external work separately. Construction-time decomposition is inexpensive relative to repeatedly rebuilding bodies; no frame-cost claim is established here.

**Rejecting test.** Propose an isolated, gravity-free impulse check on the actual constructed body: verify `Δv = j/m`, `Δω = I_world⁻¹(r × j)` and corresponding kinetic-energy change against independent double calculations. Repeat with rotated orientation and an off-centre mass. Check total cart mass includes four rollers without incorrectly welding them into deck inertia. Existing `tests/gravity_cart_integration_tests.cpp:46` rejects invalid bank/power/force values and verifies physical arrival; it does not alone validate inertia or whole-system energy conservation.

## E. Finite reciprocal foot traction with contact effective mass

**Classification: experimental but practical.** ScraperX's reduced-order foot actuation is present in production source; its gravity-based normal-load proxy needs targeted limits rather than claims of exact Coulomb contact.

**Problem and owner.** The capsule has zero solver friction to avoid wall sticking. On causal routes, finite native feet must brake/accelerate and oppose downhill gravity while reacting on the contacted machine. `src/sim/simulation.cpp:5538` owns `apply_ground_contact_force`; `:5597` owns recovery intent. `src/sim/mechanism_kit.cpp:271` owns welded-cluster inverse mass used by departure. Jolt remains the normal-contact authority.

**Mathematics and assumptions.** Project velocity/force onto the tangent plane using `P = 1 − nnᵀ`. Current correction uses `F = m(v_desired − Pu)/0.18 s − mPg`, clipped by approximately `μ mg max(0.3,n_y)`; μ is 0.8 on causal supports and 0.85 otherwise. This load estimate is not a measured solver normal impulse, especially on accelerating platforms. For normalized force direction t, `k = J M⁻¹ Jᵀ = 1/m_player + 1/m_support + (r × t)·I_support⁻¹(r × t)` in kg⁻¹, with player rotation suppressed. A constant impulse j costs `W = (t·u)j + 0.5 k j²`; positive work is capped at `3000 W × dt`. Separate dissipative braking from positive acceleration. True Coulomb friction instead bounds tangential impulse by `|j_t| ≤ μ j_n`.

**Verified APIs and native approach.** Installed `ContactListener.h:30` defines manifold normal direction; `BodyInterface.h:220–221` has COM/point `AddForce`; `Body.h:209` provides world inverse inertia. Keep normal collision in Jolt and foot intent in the existing force owner, applying `−F` at the dynamic support point. A future measured-normal-load variant must obtain attributable normal impulses from the actual contact path before changing the cap. Raising capsule friction would also affect walls; a second collision solver would create competing authority. Custom feet offer bounded intentional locomotion but require explicit slope/platform assumptions.

**Expected behavior, risks and cost.** Neutral feet hold a supported moderate incline, weak traction permits slip, and a light plate reacts instead of silently supplying unlimited acceleration. Risks include stale support, large mass ratios, zero effective mass, float rounding of caps and reaction torque omitted at offset contact. Record force, slip, work, support acceleration and peak cap utilization; separate command prediction from solver energy. One force pair and a support lock occur per relevant tick; measure contention before replacing synchronization.

**Rejecting test.** Inspected `tests/physical_traversal_tests.cpp:152` stages real tread contact, then rejects a first-tick acceleration outside `(0,0.10)` m/s or absent positive footwork. Propose moderate and overloaded incline cases, plus moving/rotating supports, verifying tangent-force limits and equal/opposite force/torque before `Update`. Repeat with neutral input after recovery expires. Arrival alone cannot reject an unlimited force or a hidden velocity assignment.

## H. Respect float precision while preserving 90 Hz and one render timestamp

**Classification: production-established** for fixed scheduling/interpolation; a large-world precision migration is **research-stage or high-risk** until an observed defect requires it.

**Problem and owner.** Upper-tower thin contacts and float impulse receipts require scale-aware tolerances, while camera, hands and support mesh must render the same simulated instant. `src/sim/simulation.hpp:474` sets 90 Hz; `src/sim/simulation.cpp:8932` owns the double accumulator and `:8972` interpolates player positions. `:8990` resolves rendered hand anchors. The bridge at `src/bridge/scraperx_simulation.cpp:714` forwards stepping; it does not own another simulation. Jolt stepping at `src/sim/simulation.cpp:2349` uses `Update` with four collision steps when the slingshot owner exists, otherwise one.

**Mathematics and assumptions.** `h = 1/90 s`, `N = floor((remainder + frame_dt + epsilon)/h)`, and `alpha = remainder/h`. Render position is `(1−alpha)x_previous + alpha x_current`; orientation needs quaternion interpolation in the owning render path. This introduces up to one tick of presentation lag. Installed build cache has `DOUBLE_PRECISION=OFF`; `Jolt/Math/Real.h:26` therefore aliases `RVec3` to float `Vec3`. IEEE binary32 position spacing is `2^(floor(log2|x|)−23)` metres: about 0.0000305 m near 319 m and 0.0000610 m near 1,000 m. Solver slop and model approximation can exceed these spacings. Double receipt arithmetic cannot recover precision already lost in native float velocity additions.

**Verified APIs and native approach.** Installed `PhysicsSystem.h:162` declares `Update(float,int,TempAllocator*,JobSystem*)`; there is no assumed `Step()` API. Retain the current rate and accumulator, evaluate player/support/hand transforms using the same alpha, and inspect actual float responses before charging actuator work. Changing to double positions or rebasing the world affects coordinate boundaries, caches, authored geometry and extension compatibility; benchmark a demonstrated need first. Increasing collision steps increases collision/solver work and cannot fix an incorrect receipt. Fixed time does not establish cross-platform determinism; the inspected cache has `CROSS_PLATFORM_DETERMINISTIC=OFF`.

**Expected behavior, risks and cost.** Equal tick-aligned input yields equal authoritative progression under different render pacing; planted hands stay attached visually. Risks include mixed raw/interpolated poses, restart history discontinuity, tiny increments rounding to zero and long frame catch-up. Source accepts up to 3,600 seconds in one call, potentially 324,000 ticks; this is an exposed scheduling limit, not a measured shipping hitch. Log steps/frame, remainder, alpha, ULP-scaled velocity change, rejected impulses and step-time percentiles without claiming unmeasured performance.

**Rejecting test.** Inspected `tests/simulation_tests.cpp:719` compares 90 individual ticks with a one-second batch to 1e−6 m and rejects negative/NaN time without state mutation. Extend with identical tick-indexed inputs and 30/60/120 Hz frame partitions, a half-tick rotating grip, and restart: reject hand/support separation and interpolation through old positions. A float boundary probe must compare actual native additions with the receipt, including sub-ULP impulses and speed/work caps. Do not turn the saved 318.98 m recovery discrepancy into a precision diagnosis without replay evidence that separates contact geometry, solver slop and pose reporting.
