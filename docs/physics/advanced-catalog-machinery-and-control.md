## C — Compliant physical hands and articulated supports

**Problem, ownership and classification.** A climb must load its actual moving support, retain gravity and preserve departure momentum. A root-follow animation can satisfy a height check while bypassing all three. The existing owner is [PhysicalHandClimb](../../src/sim/physical_hand_climb.cpp), especially `replace` at line 26, rigid constraint creation at line 50 and reaction readback at line 268. Native `vertical::Machine::hinge`/`slider` in [vertical_machine.cpp](../../src/sim/supplied/vertical_machine.cpp), lines 79/89, provide actual articulated supports. Classification: **Production-established** constraint formulation; ScraperX's route and device acceptance remain scoped to existing evidence.

**Mathematics.** For a translation coordinate, let error `e = x_target − x_relative` in metres and relative speed `v` in m/s. An unsaturated spring applies `F = k e − c v`; `k = 5000 N/m` and acquisition damping `c = 180 N·s/m` per rigid hand are CHOSEN. Each axis is capped at approximately `1500/√3 = 866.0254 N`, bounding the three-axis force norm by 1500 N per hand. This cube sacrifices diagonal-independent strength for a guaranteed vector bound. Two vertical springs against a fixed support give DERIVED equilibrium sag `mg/(2k) ≈ 0.0834 m` for the actual 85 kg player and 9.81 m/s² gravity. Dynamic support response depends on constraint effective mass `(J M⁻¹ Jᵀ)⁻¹`, including support rotation; using player mass alone to tune damping is only a fixed-support approximation.

**Verified API and native approach.** Installed [SixDOFConstraint.h](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/e77f175595e64cb44218cc9d9d56fc365ad0e36a/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/SixDOFConstraint.h), lines 86/90/172/185/198, defines free/fixed axes, `SetMotorState`, `SetTargetPositionCS` and `GetTotalLambdaMotorTranslation`. [SpringSettings.h](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/e77f175595e64cb44218cc9d9d56fc365ad0e36a/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/SpringSettings.h) defines `StiffnessAndDamping` with dimensional gains. Preserve the existing two-body constraint and its constraint-space target. Regripping adjusts target and anchor together to retain extension; detachment removes the constraint without a velocity write. Read force as impulse divided by the actual collision-substep duration, with the existing player/body2 sign convention.

**Alternatives, behavior and failures.** A fixed joint removes useful compliance and may overconstrain two hands; direct reciprocal forces require correctly transformed anchors, torque and stability management. The existing spring should sag under weight, react on a dynamic beam and release with current momentum. Wrong frame conversion causes slipping grips; using a full tick for substep lambda underreports force fourfold; regrip target resets can create free work. The cargo-net branch intentionally uses reciprocal material-point impulses rather than treating the deforming net as a rigid SixDOF anchor.

**Instrumentation, cost and rejecting test.** Log separation, spring error, each hand's force, support reaction and acquisition/release velocities. Existing per-constraint 40 velocity/8 position iterations cost solver time; measure the loaded island before altering them. [physical_hand_climb_tests.cpp](../../tests/physical_hand_climb_tests.cpp), line 127, rejects wrong equilibrium with 2 mm sag and 2 N weight-reaction tolerances and checks that detach leaves instantaneous velocity unchanged. Its dynamic-support cases reject missing recoil. These are useful native falsifiers, not proof of touch comfort or every articulated-machine transfer.

## D — Finite powered reset, with hydraulics only when justified

**Problem, ownership and classification.** The slab-haul cart rises from falling mass and needs paid reset/recall. A force rating alone does not limit power or energy, and accounting only net kinetic gain can hide positive work after braking a reversing load. [SuppliedAscent](../../src/sim/supplied_ascent.cpp), `cart_impulse` at line 39 and `step_cart` at line 174, owns the current controller. [gravity_cart_geometry.hpp](../../src/sim/gravity_cart_geometry.hpp), line 21, owns CHOSEN 200 kJ capacity, 10 kW power, 9 kN slab authority and 120 N·m release authority. Classification: **Experimental but practical** production controller with focused native/touch receipts and incomplete whole-assembly energy closure. No hydraulic pressure-state owner is present in this cart.

**Mathematics.** Along a unit actuation axis, let `u` be initial projected velocity and `κ` the projected inverse mass or inverse inertia. An impulse `j ≥ 0` gives `ΔK = u j + ½κj²`. Linear `j` is N·s; angular `j` is N·m·s; both energy terms are joules. Positive work is `∫₀ʲ max(0,u+κs) ds`. For reversal `u < 0 < u+κj`, it equals `(u+κj)²/(2κ)`, while absorbed work is `u²/(2κ)`. Their difference is net `ΔK`; their positive term remains payable. The current source debit is conservative charged positive work divided by CHOSEN efficiency `.85`, plus a shared 80 W startup overhead times substep `h`. The latch and slab share `min(E_remaining,10000h)` joules; impulse also obeys rating times `h` and measured velocity limits.

**Verified API and native approach.** [Body.h](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/e77f175595e64cb44218cc9d9d56fc365ad0e36a/Jolt/Physics/Body/Body.h), lines 213/221, supplies `AddImpulse`/`AddAngularImpulse`; [MotionProperties.h](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/e77f175595e64cb44218cc9d9d56fc365ad0e36a/Jolt/Physics/Body/MotionProperties.h), line 140, supplies `MultiplyWorldSpaceInverseInertiaByVector`. The owner wakes and validates real bodies, computes the bounded impulse, validates actual float additions and debits before delivery. The slider motor stays Off; its finite friction brakes passively. Do not replace this controller with an unaccounted velocity motor.

**Alternatives, behavior and failures.** The simpler Jolt force-limited velocity motor is suitable when only capacity matters, but does not itself enforce this source ledger. A hydraulic comparison would require `F = PA`, `v = Q/A`, `P_hydraulic = P Q` and `P_mechanical = Fv`, with Pa, m², m³/s and watts. Those are conditional design equations, not existing cart state. Add pressure dynamics only for a visible actuator whose load/flow response creates a useful player decision. Current expected behavior is unpaid gravity ascent, paid recall and finite cutoff. Reversal accounting, float rounding, source depletion, slack tow and a loaded retainer are consequential edges; passive brake heat must never refill the source.

**Instrumentation, cost and rejecting test.** Existing snapshots expose bank, power, force, positive work, heat, overdraft and actuator rounding residual. Bounded retry loops are constant-size; measure their worst substep cost before optimizing. [gravity_cart_integration_tests.cpp](../../tests/gravity_cart_integration_tests.cpp), lines 45/175/213/244, rejects overdraft, power/force excess, charged gravity ascent and unpaid recall/reset. An additional proposed impulse-level reversal case must reject charging only net `ΔK`; it has not been implemented here. [Cart evidence](../../evidence/supplied-machines-2026-10-08/cart-player/README.md) records a real-player route, not full force/energy closure or phone performance.

## F — Passive rollers, real rail load and terrain relevance

**Problem, ownership and classification.** The cart's rollers must carry and roll the loaded chassis; a guide that carries normal weight would create decorative wheels. [build_gravity_cart](../../src/sim/gravity_cart_geometry.cpp), lines 215–247, owns the 275 kg chassis, four separate 35 kg rollers of radius `.28 m`, planar guide, 570 kg slab and tension-only tow. Classification: **Experimental but practical** rail machinery. This is a constrained industrial cart, not an existing Jolt vehicle/suspension/tire integration.

**Mathematics.** Under ideal no-slip rolling, `ω = v/R` and `I = ½mR²` for each solid-cylinder roller; reflected rotational mass `I/R² = m/2`, giving DERIVED 70 kg across four rollers. The actual shaped inertia remains Jolt-authoritative. With along-track coordinate `q`, slab height `y_s(q)` and `α = −dy_s/dq`, gravity drive is `Q_g = m_s g α − m_cart_loaded g sin(60°)`; reflected slab mass is `m_s α²`. The two variable rope legs determine `α`, so a nominal 1:1 pulley ratio does not prove identical cart/slab displacement at every pose. For each passive roller, required tangential force is approximately `I a/R²` and must satisfy `|f_i| ≤ μN_i`, with `N_i ≥ 0`. The cart tow supplies uphill work; static traction need not support the entire downhill gravity component by itself.

**Verified API and native approach.** Installed [HingeConstraint.h](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/e77f175595e64cb44218cc9d9d56fc365ad0e36a/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/HingeConstraint.h), lines 39/50/119, verifies authored axes, angular limits and MotorOff. SixDOF free/fixed-axis APIs leave XY translation and Z pitch free while preventing out-of-plane escape. [Kit::connect_rope](../../src/sim/mechanism_kit.cpp), line 757, uses installed [PulleyConstraintSettings](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/e77f175595e64cb44218cc9d9d56fc365ad0e36a/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/PulleyConstraint.h), setting minimum length zero and finite maximum length. Preserve actual cylinder/rail contacts, free roller hinges and the existing tension-only rope; do not add a second tire-force solver over those contacts.

**Alternatives, behavior and failures.** A track-axis slider is cheaper but can carry weight without wheels; Jolt vehicle tire/suspension machinery is unnecessary for the current fixed rail route. Expected behavior is rolling under actual contact load, tow slack without compressive pushing and real stop/retainer capture. Rail seams, unloading wheels, insufficient friction, COM shifts and obstacle contact can cause slip or derailment. The [component receipt](../../evidence/supplied-machines-2026-10-08/cart-geometry/README.md) records zero XY/pitch guide reaction and sampled rail contacts, but also a failed-tow loaded-retainer release that stalls at 120 N·m. That unresolved fault must remain visible; intact production reset passes a different case.

**Instrumentation, cost and rejecting test.** Sample roller contact IDs/normals, guide lambda, chassis pitch, `v−Rω`, tow tension and retainer angle. Four rigid rollers add contact/hinge work; no measured vehicle-budget improvement is claimed. Existing component receipts support load-path inspection and the real-player route rejects fake footing. A proposed causal regression should remove rail collision in an isolated fixture and require loss of normal support; immobilizing rollers should increase slip/work. These rejecting tests are not yet maintained tests. Uneven terrain, soil, tracks and suspension remain deferred until gameplay requires them.

## G — Rated joint-space hold and controlled acceleration

**Problem, ownership and classification.** A stationary hoist must resist gravity through its rating, while release of the pendant must stop queued motion. The regression-kernel jib is owned by [simulation.cpp](../../src/sim/simulation.cpp), constants at line 409, construction at line 2952 and `update_jib` at line 4257. It is a retained fixture, not current campaign ascent. Classification: **Production-established** PD/spring control; machine-specific tuning and commercial usability require bounded evidence.

**Mathematics.** The captured extension becomes a fixed target on transition to neutral. The approximate joint force is `clamp(k(x_target−x)−c ẋ,−20000,20000) N`, with CHOSEN `k=500000 N/m` and `c=50000 N·s/m`. For fixed-anchor 1240 kg hook/load, DERIVED `ω_n=√(k/m)≈20.08 rad/s`, `ζ=c/(2√(km))≈1.004`, and static sag `mg/k≈.0243 m`. These are unsaturated one-coordinate estimates, not exact coupled-jib eigenvalues. Saturation correctly prevents lifting overweight loads; increasing gains cannot overcome capacity.

**Verified API and native approach.** Installed [SliderConstraint.h](https://github.com/jrouwe/JoltPhysics/blob/e77f175595e64cb44218cc9d9d56fc365ad0e36a/Jolt/Physics/Constraints/SliderConstraint.h), lines 96/107/109/111/129, verifies position measurement, motor modes, targets and lambda. `MotorSettings::SetForceLimit` enforces the same movement/hold rating. Existing native code captures measured extension once when entering Position mode, retains it while neutral, and restores mode/targets with checkpoints. Separately, [Machine::pre_step](../../src/sim/supplied/vertical_machine.cpp), line 25, uses position-to-velocity gain `2 s⁻¹` plus hinge acceleration bounds in rad/s²; this is an outer proportional loop around Jolt's velocity motor, not a custom torque PD solver.

**Alternatives, behavior and failures.** Gravity feedforward could reduce sag; integral control requires anti-windup when force saturates. LQR/MPC lacks an established need here. Re-latching the target each tick would allow load creep; restoring bodies without the target would cause post-restart motion. Expected behavior is finite sag, settling after station departure, real limits and overload refusal. No finite-energy source ledger is established for this kernel jib's powered hold.

**Instrumentation, cost and rejecting test.** Log extension error, velocity, lambda/substep force and saturation duration; controllers cost O(drives), while coupled solver work dominates. [simulation_tests.cpp](../../tests/simulation_tests.cpp), lines 1726/1784/1810/1817, rejects continued off-station lifting, overweight rise above 1 cm and stale hold targets after restart; restored force remains bounded by 20001 N. Retain those meaningful tests. New damping/acceleration tuning should compare settle time and overshoot under the same load/input trace before adopting more elaborate control.
