# Macro mechanism ideas — corrected design catalogue

Reworked from the owner's supplied Colossus Sequence and earlier lift ideas, 2026-09-26. These are **design options**, not verified blueprints or a mandatory eleven-stage course. The Atlas owns actual layout. Large visible objects must do useful work and leave playable aftermath; small release parts must not become the main activity.

## Colossus: what survives and what changes

| Supplied phase | Keep | Necessary correction / better handoff |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Keystone avalanche | A contained rack of massive rolling pipes is a clear finite gravity source. | A 10 kg ball cannot be assumed to extract a loaded wedge. Derive actual retainer load/friction and release work; use a visible accessible release. Model hollow-pipe inertia. Bound the pile, catch width and spill states; “chaotic but deterministic” is not a robustness proof. |
| 2. Pan balance | A huge beam and visibly accumulating ballast. | Compare moments over the stroke, not just which basket exceeds 1,000 kg. Include beam/pan/rider mass, COMs, arm lengths and constraint loads. Prefer the beam itself becoming a stable bridge, with real receiving seats, over a tiny hooked cable chase. |
| 3. Trapdoor and boulder | A large hinged floor releases stored gravitational energy. | The loaded support pin may bind or require more force than the source can supply. Use a dimensioned visible support/release, clear hinge sweep and a broad funnel. Grade does not hide the boulder's missing drop volume. |
| 4. Wrecking pendulum | Boulder contact sends a suspended massive body through a readable arc. | Strike the receiver before the turning point: speed is zero at an ideal apex. Derive contact normal, effective inertia, restitution/loss and swing energy. A rigid link and native hinge can represent the rod; it is not an elastic chain. Capture the boulder afterward. |
| 5. Monolith dominoes | Several huge slabs visibly relay falling potential energy. | Their spacing must follow actual collision geometry and each COM tipping barrier. Each upright slab is another preloaded energy source; momentum alone does not fund the row. Constrain the fall lane with physical geometry. Prefer a final broad stair/ramp or seated span over narrowly hitting an unseen trigger. |
| 6. Giant seesaw | A heavy falling slab drives an unequal-arm lever. | Lever speed gain trades against force; it does not multiply energy. A 500 kg payload needs 4.905 kJ per metre of rise before losses. Model impact, beam inertia and release contact, then bound the landing envelope. For first ascent, use a guided ballast delivery or a bridge that seats rather than launching the player. |
| 7. Switchbacks | Huge ramps, obvious turns and visible dissipative impacts. | Gravity reaccelerates after each collision; low restitution alone does not guarantee a fixed exit speed. A 50 m ramp at 15 degrees drops 12.94 m; five consume about 64.7 m of height before inter-ramp gaps. Exit speed depends on the last ramp, rolling inertia and losses. Use fewer ramps sized to measured incoming energy; provide spill containment and explicit bumper compression/braking. |
| 8. Newton's cradle | An optional conspicuous equal-mass collision relay. | Five constrained spheres at restitution 0.98 do not guarantee a perfect one-in/one-out result in a rigid-body solver. Approach alignment, contact tolerances, simultaneous-contact solve and support geometry matter. Do not claim the solver models a steel compression wave. A cradle redirects existing energy; one direct pendulum is simpler when relay adds no player value. |
| 9. Cable spool | A heavy rolling drum can unwind a taut cable and do useful lifting work. | Reject “chain snaps taut, loose ring shoots at latch.” It gives no defined ring constraint, launch direction or reliable energy transfer and risks huge arrest impulses. Keep the cable engaged through a dimensioned wrap/radius and finite brake, driving a broad lever or guided carriage continuously. Account for rotational kinetic energy and descent. |
| 10. Scissor jack | A large visible extensible linkage can push across a gap. | A pantograph adds geometry and joint losses; it supplies no energy. Derive spring preload, force vs extension, singular configurations and the recharge source. Replace it with a plain spring-backed plunger or falling-weight ram when scissors add no meaningful capability. The output force must match the receiving lever's useful torque direction. |
| 11. High striker | A large weighted lever can drive a visible vertical slider and ring a gong. | Raising 100 kg by 100 m requires at least 98.1 kJ and a ballistic launch speed above 44.3 m/s once losses and residual strike speed count. At its apex it cannot deliver a meaningful impact. Lower the travel or increase source work honestly; strike below the apex. A horizontal ram cannot push a downward-facing pad without an actual bell crank. Prefer the gong releasing a plainly visible gravity drawbridge whose receiver is already built. |

## Adapted chains worth prototyping

**Pipe rack → balance bridge → landing.** The opening Atlas candidate. The player releases a large rack, sees pipes load the short arm, watches a long deck seat against a fixed +8 m receiver, then climbs it. It ends in useful geometry rather than another miniature release. Release, stopping and recovery are still unresolved design work.

**Rolling drum → cable → guided carriage.** A drum loses height while a continuously tensioned cable raises a platform. Use a real wrap model or valid pulley relation, include drum rotational energy and a finite brake, and provide fixed boarding/arrival supports. Show the whole load path. This is an alternative to the balance bridge, not an additional mandatory lift.

**Heavy roller → pendulum → seated span.** A broad trough delivers a roller into a large pendulum, which pushes a hinged deck onto a receiving corbel while it still has useful speed. Keep collision normals, arrest and miss landing visible. The roller and pendulum remain in their physical spent states.

**Falling monoliths → broad stair.** Successive slabs become supported inclined surfaces or steps after tipping. Each rest pose must be stable under the player and have usable risers/landings. Avoid converting thin slabs into invisible walkable ramps. Upright slabs must have a credible initial support and a finite reset method.

**Counterweight → lever → drawbridge.** A large suspended mass lowers, turns a visible walking beam and lowers a bridge onto a fixed seat. No elaborate interchangeable shackle inventory is necessary unless changing that connection offers a meaningful alternate solution. Mass, travel and damping govern the result.

## Shared calculations and verification

For rolling, use `m g Δh = 1/2 m v² + 1/2 I ω² + losses`; `v = rω` only for no slip. For a hollow pipe use its actual inner/outer radii. A lever obeys work balance and changing moments; include every moved body's COM and inertia. A pendulum has `m g L(1 − cos θ)` of rise energy from its low point, before losses. A spring's energy is its force integral (or `1/2 k x²` only for a linear spring). Impact restitution is relative normal separation/approach speed, not an energy multiplier.

Define one native owner for every body/constraint and finite force law. No animation driving consequential poses, no chain of area-entered success flags, no unlimited joint motor, and no arbitrary post-impact velocity assignment. Test actual player access, handoff and retry along with no-source/no-link failure and perturbed initial placement. Repeatability claims name engine build and platform. Device visual readability cannot be inferred from headless logs.
